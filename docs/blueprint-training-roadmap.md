# From blueprint pilot to a trained poker bot

## Objective and scope

Get a six-player, 100 BB, no-rake bot that reliably earns chips against the existing scripted style pool. That is the repository's v0.5 strength target. A stronger human-reference claim is a later v1.0 target. We will take ideas from Pluribus where they help, but the result matters more than reproducing its architecture.

The [first M4 pilot](reports/blueprint-pilot-v1.md) established legal play, exact resume, and a working arena. Its two iterations visited only 408 abstract information sets; at a fixed first preflop decision just 2/169 hand classes had trained entries. [Trainer scaling](blueprint-scaling.md) removed the full-table iteration copy and per-iteration compression. The [one-hour M4 slice](reports/blueprint-m4-slice-v1.md) reached 5.83 million entries. The [RunPod sizing check](reports/blueprint-runpod-sizing-v1.md) then found that more workers slow the current trainer and that trained lookups remain rare against different opponent styles. The [summary-history comparison](reports/blueprint-history-summary-v1.md) used fewer entries and improved self-play lookup rates, but did not materially improve held-out style-pool coverage. The owner now wants a sustained checkpoint-0.4 run against random play before the v0.5 scripted-pool gate. The next decision is how to start that run with substantial memory and a frozen progress chart, not another short abstraction sweep.

## Route to the first serious campaign

| Step | Work and exit decision |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Make the existing trainer scalable | Preserve a fixed policy during each six-role iteration without a full-table copy per iteration; checkpoint at a bounded wall-clock interval and on clean shutdown rather than every iteration. Keep atomic iteration publication, exact resume, legal-observation inference, and one focused recovery check. Add peak RSS, table growth, nodes/second, checkpoint time/size, disk use, and trained-versus-fallback decision counts. Before paying for many AWS CPUs, let workers traverse independent samples against the same frozen policy and combine their deltas at the iteration boundary; check that parallel and serial results remain valid. Do not start an architecture sweep. |
| 2. Start seed 1 on the M4 | Choose the six-player abstraction and sampling recipe intended for the long run. Run one capped, approximately one-hour slice with an **approximately 10 GiB process RSS ceiling** and a disk guard; leave memory for the OS and other work on the 16 GiB machine. Measure entry growth and throughput late in the slice, not only at startup. Save a resumable checkpoint and coverage report; add a small validation result if exporting the policy fits the measured RAM headroom. If the recipe stays fixed, this slice is the beginning of seed 1, not a disposable toy test. Weak poker results do not fail this step; illegal actions, non-finite values, failed recovery, or resource limits do. |
| 3. Resolve the measured architecture risk | Complete. The 64-GB RunPod check found one worker fastest and sparse held-out coverage. A same-work M4 comparison found that a shorter public-history key improved sampled training lookups but not held-out style-pool lookups at 8,733 iterations. Keep the original key as the reference. The next sustained run may reveal a training or memory bound; do not delay it for another key sweep. A many-core rental still needs measured worker scaling to justify its cores. |
| 4. Start sustained checkpoint-0.4 training | Agree with the owner on a host and RAM ceiling that can hold a much larger table; use RunPod first if its available tiers and cost fit. Freeze one training recipe, random-opponent evaluation schedule, checkpoint cadence, work/time/resource guards, artifact-retention path, and spending ceiling. Evaluate saved checkpoints on the same random-opponent distribution with fresh paired deals and uncertainty, and plot BB/100 alongside training work, coverage and memory. Continue through weak early estimates. The 0.4 result is a learning curve and a recoverable player benchmark, not the v0.5 scripted-pool qualification. After observing a real resource or learning bound, decide whether the next serious run needs more RAM, faster traversal, a new representation or search. |
| 5. Judge 0.4 and plan v0.5 | Export the retained checkpoints. Report trained/fallback coverage by street and position, card sensitivity, legal-action rate, random-opponent BB/100 with uncertainty over training work, total compute, peak RAM, and inference latency. If the learning curve rises, continue or scale the recipe toward the stronger scripted-pool target. For v0.5, train at least two independent seeds and run **one fresh, predeclared confirmation** on paired held-out scripted-pool deals, with multiplicity-adjusted lower confidence bounds above zero for each seed. No repeated attempts on the same final test. |
| 6. If 0.4 learning stalls | Diagnose the largest observed failure from coverage and hand traces. Make one targeted improvement and one comparable run. Likely candidates are better sampling/abstraction or observation-safe range-aware postflop search. Preserve the 0.4 run as a baseline. |
| 7. Make a compact local player | Once a strong larger player is demonstrated, derive an M4/16-GiB variant by compressing or pruning its table, or by training a smaller policy to imitate its decisions. Measure inference RSS, latency, legality and the strength lost on the same held-out arena. Keep the larger player as the reference; a compact artifact is a separate evaluated model, not an automatic result of running independent seeds. |

Checkpoint 0.4 is earned when a sustained run has retained checkpoints, a rising random-opponent validation trend over substantial training work, and a positive lower 95% confidence bound on one fresh final random-opponent test. A measured plateau or failure to beat random is valuable campaign evidence but does not earn the checkpoint. The later v0.5 campaign still requires independent seeds and the scripted-pool strength check. The frozen [0.4 campaign](blueprint-checkpoint-04.md) includes scripted-opponent validation and actual preflop/flop trained-decision coverage in TensorBoard.

## RAM and spending rule

The M4 is the default for coding checks, recovery, the one-hour scaling slice, and any subsequent training that continues to fit. Run only one large seed process at a time on its 16 GiB RAM. A 10 GiB process ceiling is an initial guard, not evidence that a 10 GiB table will stay within it indefinitely; require a growth projection and watch the live RSS. A checkpoint must survive any guard stop.

The one-worker M4 slice reached 5.83 million entries at 4.24 GiB peak RSS. Its observed growth suggests roughly 10–12 million entries might remain below the 10 GiB **training-process** guard, but that range is only a planning estimate: dictionary growth is uneven. Export of the saved 5.83-million-entry checkpoint peaked at 7.33 GiB process RSS on RunPod; the current and average compressed policies occupied 162 MB and 116 MB. This one size point does not establish a safe linear extrapolation to 128–512 GB. A future large player and a compact 16-GB player may use different representations and must each earn their own playing-strength result.

The owner-reported RunPod console prices on 2026-09-24 are **$0.44/hour for 8 vCPU/64 GB** and **$0.88/hour for 16 vCPU/128 GB** in the 3 GHz memory-optimized tier, plus disk charges. The completed check was stopped within its $3 ceiling, with $17.83 displayed immediately afterward. The owner delegated budgeting for bounded RunPod tests; record each live quote, full lifetime, storage and settled cost. Set a separate reviewed budget for the long campaign from measured throughput and memory rather than assuming the current balance or the paper's cost estimate funds it.

AWS [lists `r7i.16xlarge` as 64 vCPUs and 512 GiB](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/instance-types/memory-optimized/), but [those 64 vCPUs are 32 physical cores with two threads each](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/cpu-options-supported-instances-values.html). The quoted **$1.13/hour Spot** rate is an unverified snapshot, not a campaign rate: [AWS says Spot prices vary by capacity and region](https://aws.amazon.com/ec2/spot/pricing/). The quoted $70–200 or $150–250 reproduction estimates are hypotheses, not our budget. Do not derive our runtime by dividing Pluribus's core-hours by 64 vCPUs, or assume a modern CPU makes our Python implementation faster than theirs. Use measured nodes or completed iterations per hour at each worker count, then calculate the two-seed budget from the selected AWS quote plus EBS, retained checkpoint storage, and evaluation.

For Spot, checkpoint at an interval whose recomputation cost is acceptable, write atomically to persistent storage, and verify that a fresh instance can load the latest retained checkpoint. [AWS gives roughly two minutes of interruption notice](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/spot-best-practices.html), which is too little to rely on for an unmeasured large-table save. [EBS can persist after instance termination](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/AWSEC2/latest/UserGuide/ec2-instance-lifecycle.html); configure that behavior deliberately and account for its continuing charges. An interruption resumes from the last durable checkpoint within the frozen work and spend caps.

The [Pluribus paper](https://noambrown.github.io/papers/19-Science-Superhuman.pdf) reports about **$144 in 2019 cloud spot prices** for its blueprint: eight days, 12,400 CPU core-hours on a 64-core server, and less than 512 GB training RAM. Its **128 GB** limit is for playing with the compressed blueprint, not its training RAM. Pluribus then used real-time search after the first betting round, so its final playing strength cannot be attributed to blueprint training alone. Those figures do not price our Python trainer or guarantee that a 16-vCPU/128-GB rental can reproduce Pluribus on the same schedule. The budget should be based on our measured run, with storage and evaluation included.

## Deliberately few gates

The recovery check, M4 slice, bounded RunPod measurement and single public-history comparison are complete. Move to the sustained 0.4 run after its host, work, guards and spending are agreed. Correctness and resource failures stop the run immediately. A negative interim poker estimate does not trigger another sequence of tiny tuning runs. The v0.5 strength gate remains firm because otherwise we cannot tell whether training produced a decent bot.

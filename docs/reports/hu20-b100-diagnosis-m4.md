# B100M HU20 decision diagnosis on the M4

Draft [PR #117](https://github.com/dberweger2017/deepcfr-texas-no-limit-holdem-6-players/pull/117) evaluates the saved, unchanged uncapped HU20 policies. The [protocol](../hu20-b100-diagnosis-protocol.md) fixed decision selection, conditional worlds, a fresh LBR schedule, and lookup-only action translation before their outcomes were opened. This is research evidence for the declared 20BB game, not model promotion or a v0.5/v1.0 qualification.

**Conclusion:** more work from 20M to 100M again improved target profit against the bounded LBR on a fresh paired schedule, but the 40M/80M/100M increments are too imprecise to establish continued improvement near 100M. Conditional action gaps occur at both sparse and heavily visited keys. A small same-key card probe suggests some concrete-hand differences, but its suit-equivalent preflop negative control also produced an apparent difference. The declared uniform compatible opponent range is not the posterior induced by the actual LBR history. These results do **not** yet identify a training change with enough confidence. The one recommended next step is a small posterior-conditioned, information-safe measurement on fresh outcome-blind trained decisions. It belongs on the M4; no paid host or training run is proposed here.

## What the conditional decisions say

Under the **declared uniform compatible opponent holding range**, the held-out selected-action gain averaged 3.71 BB per selected decision. The per-decision 95% lower bound was above zero in 36/60 cases. For the 48 trained keys, the mean was 2.92 BB and 24 had a positive lower bound; for the deliberately oversampled 12 fallback decisions, all 12 did. Those counts are descriptive across selected, correlated situations, not a prevalence estimate for ordinary play or a multiple-test-adjusted discovery rate. In #116's actual B100M LBR hands, fallback occurred only 41 times in 28,549 target decisions; oversampling it here must not turn it into the explanation for the full-game loss.

The street pattern is uneven: positive per-decision lower bounds occurred in 2/12 preflop, 8/15 flop, 15/18 turn and 11/15 river cases. Median selected-key visits were 406, 1,101, 52 and 58 respectively. Among trained keys with at least 100 updates, **18/36** still had a positive held-out gap; among those with at least 1,000, **6/12** did. Sparse later-street refinement is plausible, but more visits alone is not a complete explanation under this model. Visit count, regrets and policy entropy are retained per decision; correlation does not establish why the saved action is weak.

The optional same-key probe selected the lowest-hash trained root per street before looking at values, then the low/high rank compatible concrete holdings that shared its key and saved distribution. At a flop key with **623 visits**, the high-hand minus low-hand pot-versus-saved-min value contrast was +4.35 BB [exploratory 95% 0.55, 8.16]. At a river key with **41 visits**, the analogous pot-versus-saved-check contrast was −6.94 [−12.02, −1.85]. These demonstrate materially different **sampled conditional values** within a key under the declared model; they do not establish that changing the bucket would improve trained play.

An important negative control limits that inference. Two preflop `K8o` suit assignments share a key and are strategically suit-isomorphic before any board; nevertheless their sampled pot-versus-call contrast was +5.71 [0.27, 11.15] BB. That apparent difference cannot be a real card-abstraction distinction in the suit-symmetric game. The four roots and 13 exploratory action contrasts have no multiplicity correction, and each holding has only 48 held-out worlds. The [all-action contrast file](hu20-b100-diagnosis-m4-artifacts/collision-contrasts.json) preserves the warning rather than selecting only favorable card examples.

Most importantly, the primary audit **does not reconstruct the posterior induced by earlier LBR actions**. Its compatible holdings are uniform, and its future actions are fixed-policy rollouts. It asks whether a saved local action looks poor under that model. It cannot allocate the original −73.14 BB/100 whole-hand LBR loss to particular decisions, nor prove card, history, action-menu or regret-convergence causality. The 48-world held-out estimates can also be noisy for large pots. The exact late-street reference is a small test fixture, not an independent full-range validation of all 60 values.

## Exact-chip opponent-action translation

The nearest-size control changed only the saved policy's lookup label for an opponent raise; the real engine pot, stacks, action, legal bounds and settlement stayed native. Pot pressure is the key stress rule. Results are target BB/100 with paired, exploratory unadjusted 95% block intervals:

| Opponent | Exact lookup | Nearest lookup | Nearest − exact |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Pot pressure, 256 blocks | +21.84 | +23.08 | +1.24 [−5.20, 7.68] |
| One-third pot, 128 blocks | +23.12 | +16.96 | −6.16 [−13.98, 1.65] |
| Two-thirds pot, 128 blocks | +46.11 | +53.27 | +7.16 [−15.21, 29.53] |
| Native minraise, 128 blocks | +70.05 | +70.05 | 0.00 [0.00, 0.00] |
| Passive, 128 blocks | +116.60 | +116.60 | 0.00 [0.00, 0.00] |

Against pot pressure, translation was attempted on 382 target decisions but reached a trained translated key in only **53**; the exact and translated variants used fallback 351/1,535 and 329/1,538 target decisions respectively. Counts differ because play trajectories can diverge. One-third-pot fallback fell from 45 to 30 while the playing point estimate worsened. The on-menu minraise and passive controls produced identical concrete actions and returns under native replay. There were no illegal actions or missing-hand failures; missing translated keys were retained as fallback. Mean target decision time remained below 0.1 ms in every panel. This deterministic mapping has exploitable size boundaries and has **no demonstrated playing gain** on the key stress case. More hits alone are not a success criterion.

## One next recommendation and compute decision

**Do one posterior-conditioned conditional-value measurement before choosing a training intervention.** Freeze 24 fresh, hash-ranked trained B100M decisions, one per seed × street × actual position, with a prespecified visit threshold and explicit empty-cell handling. Estimate the opponent holding posterior from the declared LBR's observed actions using only public history and hypothetical holdings, never the actual hidden deal. Use independent evaluation worlds and a selection/evaluation split; include suit-permutation negative controls and an independently enumerated small river check. An outcome-free timing/precision preflight should choose one common feasible world count from 96/192/384 before any new return is opened. Keep all failed or limited decisions. This directly tests whether the current uniform-range gaps survive a model closer to the original attacker and whether the apparent same-key card contrasts replicate. It does not train a new model.

The completed 60×96-world audit took **36 minutes** at about 2.70 GiB peak RSS. At the same per-world cost, 24×384 worlds would take about **58 minutes** before posterior construction; reserve up to two M4 hours only after measuring that extra cost. Run one heavy process and retain per-decision world archives and attempt records so an interruption has an explicit partial result; there is no training checkpoint to resume. There are no training arms, nodes, table growth or checkpoint schedule to specify for a campaign we cannot yet justify. The existing B100M tables are about 1.50 million entries per seed, and the prior 3-million-entry save/export preflight peaked at 6.16 GiB on M4; these are resource evidence for a later bounded experiment, not a forecast for an unimplemented abstraction. The M4 is the right host for this measurement. No current RunPod balance is verified, no paid quote is needed for the M4-sized work, and **no rental is authorized**. After that measurement, choose and freeze exactly one training A/B or continuation with its own work, memory and recovery plan rather than inferring a bottleneck from the present limited conditional model.

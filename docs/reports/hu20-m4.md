# Fresh heads-up 20BB blueprint: M4 confirmation

**Draft PR #112, September 28, 2026.** Three independent, from-zero K1 blueprints completed the frozen 20M-node budget. The development-selected final current policy showed a **confirmed practical improvement over untrained uniform play on the declared six-opponent, fixed-stack panel**. It remains an experimental, restricted-menu heads-up player. No six-player, tournament or professional-strength claim is made, and no model is promoted.

## Frozen game and comparison

The [game protocol](../hu20-baseline.md) uses standard 52-card Hold'em with two actual seats, fresh 20 BB stacks each hand, 0.5/1 blinds, no ante or rake, native heads-up action order, and a two-raise min/pot/conditional-jam menu. Fold is absent whenever check is legal in training and inference. Native button-relative keys retain ordered abstract history and the named legacy postflop card descriptor; these features are lossy. The game and artifact versions reject incompatible old six-seat checkpoints. All three K1 trainers began with zero regrets and shared the same settings.

The [frozen plan](../../configs/blueprint/hu20-m4.json) has canonical SHA-256 `0da3cb6075d328acd748cf79a7e5f2d2422815c0574fdc3dbf7bb8bbb3908141`. The [development report](hu20-development-m4.md) and committed [decision](../../configs/blueprint/hu20-extraction-decision.json) selected final current `C` before confirmation. Training and development ran from source `d718049`; confirmation ran from `989f5c4`, whose intervening changes were a targeted test, the committed choice and documentation, with no solver or evaluation-code change. The decision file is SHA-256 `a118308ee6eda3d7371227a3c1092295c1b54b5164808d0b3078a09d723b0b91` in the campaign record.

The fresh confirmation has **4,096 independent two-position deal/rotation blocks per opponent** for each of six frozen opponents: loose-passive, loose-aggressive, tight-passive, tight-aggressive, pot-pressure and same-game uniform. Each trained seed and the untrained control face the same deals, positions and opponent assignment. The sole primary contrast is final current minus untrained uniform, averaged across the three seeds **within each block**, then equally across the six opponent types. The declared interval is two-sided 95%, clustered by independent block. Opponent-specific and crossplay intervals are diagnostic; they were not used to select a player.

The primary positive effect therefore supports **learning on this fixed panel**, not broad poker competence. ## Learning and crossplay diagnostics

Development's paired curve rose from +40.13 BB/100 at 2M nodes to +46.96 at 5M, +49.97 at 10M and +50.59 for final current at 20M, all relative to uniform on its separate 256-block-per-opponent schedule. The point estimates increase, but adjacent differences were not individually confirmed. Windowed `A` was +52.51 in development; its `A−C` difference was +1.92 [−6.20, +10.04], driven by the uniform opponent while trailing `C` on each scripted type. Thus `C` was frozen before the held-out run. `A` remains an explicit diagnostic artifact, not the selected player.

Fresh crossplay used 1,024 paired two-position blocks for each neighboring-seed opponent. The three final-current-versus-final-current absolute rates were −24.15, +7.40 and +5.96 BB/100, each with a 95% interval crossing zero. Against those same frozen final opponents, replacing the 2M-node early hero with its own 20M-node final hero gained +44.19, +46.85 and +81.42 BB/100 in the three pairs. These are diagnostic crossplay comparisons, not the primary panel effect or an equilibrium certificate.

All three final checkpoints have about 268k–271k abstract keys. On confirmation trajectories, **94–97% of decisions** found a final-current key, depending on street and seed; missing keys used an explicit uniform-on-menu fallback. Decision-weighted visit medians at reached keys were 385, 410 and 370 for the three seeds; 95–96% of reached decisions had a key with more than one traversal visit. These statistics are measured on each policy's own trajectories, which differ, and do not assert a universal visit threshold or perfect-recall CFR convergence. The final policy still uses the coarse baseline card descriptor. The independent finite hidden-game reference checks production regret traversal and atomic publication; no exact full-deck 20BB exploitability result was computed. The #108 river oracle evaluates a different conditional game and is not presented as a full-hand score here.
